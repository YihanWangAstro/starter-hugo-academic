<div class="p-title"><h2>Research Themes</h2><a class="p-more" href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=orcid%3A0000-0002-8614-8721&amp;sort=date+desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener">All 34 papers on NASA ADS &raquo;</a></div>
<div class="research-landscape">
<a class="landscape-card" href="#theme-1"><canvas class="landscape-anim" data-theme="1" aria-hidden="true"></canvas><span class="landscape-cap"><span class="lc-name">AGN Disks &amp; Tidal Disruptions</span><span class="lc-summary">Dense stellar environments — the accretion disks of active galactic nuclei (AGN) and the cores of star clusters — are crowded enough that stars and black holes routinely capture, pair, and destroy one another. I study these dynamics and the transients they produce: how objects are captured into AGN disks, how binaries form there, and how stars are tidally disrupted by both supermassive and stellar-mass black holes — turning AGN variability and tidal-disruption flares into probes of the embedded populations and the central black hole.</span><span class="lc-count">10 papers</span></span></a>
<a class="landscape-card" href="#theme-2"><canvas class="landscape-anim" data-theme="2" aria-hidden="true"></canvas><span class="landscape-cap"><span class="lc-name">Massive Black Holes &amp; GWs</span><span class="lc-summary">Massive black holes shape galactic nuclei, and dynamical interactions in dense systems are a key channel for assembling the compact-object binaries that gravitational-wave detectors observe. Using high-precision N-body and scattering simulations, I study how single and binary supermassive black holes drive stellar mergers, tidal disruptions, and hypervelocity ejections, and predict the rates, eccentricities, spins, and sky distribution of dynamically-formed black-hole and neutron-star mergers — including evidence that our own Galactic-centre black hole grew through a past merger.</span><span class="lc-count">8 papers</span></span></a>
<a class="landscape-card" href="#theme-3"><canvas class="landscape-anim" data-theme="3" aria-hidden="true"></canvas><span class="landscape-cap"><span class="lc-name">Planets in Star Clusters</span><span class="lc-summary">Most planetary systems are born in clustered environments where encounters with passing stars are common. With high-precision few-body simulations I study how flybys reshape planetary architectures — forming hot Jupiters, ejecting free-floating and binary planets, and swapping giant planets between stars — connecting cluster dynamics to the diversity of observed exoplanets.</span><span class="lc-count">6 papers</span></span></a>
<a class="landscape-card" href="#theme-4"><canvas class="landscape-anim" data-theme="4" aria-hidden="true"></canvas><span class="landscape-cap"><span class="lc-name">GRBs &amp; Transients</span><span class="lc-summary">The deaths of massive stars and the mergers of compact objects launch relativistic jets and engine-powered transients. I model their central engines (including magnetars) and multiwavelength afterglows, and bring that modelling to observational teams chasing the universe&#x27;s fastest transients — gamma-ray bursts, fast X-ray transients, fast radio bursts, dirty fireballs, and engine-driven supernovae — connecting theory to real data from facilities such as the Einstein Probe, FAST, Chandra, and the VLA.</span><span class="lc-count">8 papers</span></span></a>
<a class="landscape-card" href="#theme-5"><canvas class="landscape-anim" data-theme="5" aria-hidden="true"></canvas><span class="landscape-cap"><span class="lc-name">Software &amp; Methods</span><span class="lc-summary">Much of my research is enabled by open-source software I develop and release. These tools — a few-body gravity integrator and a gamma-ray-burst afterglow framework — emphasise accuracy and performance so the community can reproduce and build on my results.</span><span class="lc-count">2 papers</span></span></a>
